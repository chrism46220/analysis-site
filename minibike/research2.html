Known Fitment Facts

This plate IS intended for Predator 212 swaps. The vendor description explicitly says it adapts a "Tillotson, Predator, or Honda Clone engine (196cc, 212cc, or 225cc)" to the Mega Moto 80/105/E1000 frame.

Vendor documents these required modifications: 1. Fuel tank must be relocated — factory top-mount tank hits the top frame tube 2. Stock air box will not fit — needs low-profile pod filter 3. Stock exhaust will not fit — box muffler hits the frame 4. Centrifugal clutch works — slotted mounting holes allow fore-aft chain tension adjustment

Why Your Plate May Not Have Fit

Based on research, the most common fitment issues with MMFAMPBENT:

Issue Resolution
Bolt pattern mismatch with frame The plate has slotted engine-side holes for adjustment. Frame-side holes match the 79cc pattern. If bolts don't align, you may need to slot/drill the frame-side holes slightly. A round file or Dremel can open holes 1-2mm.
Engine sits too far forward/back The slotted holes allow ~1" of fore-aft adjustment. Center the crankshaft so the clutch aligns with the rear sprocket, then mark and drill.
Frame production variation Mega Moto frames had multiple production runs. Some builders report needing to drill 2-3 new holes. This is uncommon but possible.
Plate orientation The plate is "bent" — one side sits higher. Confirm orientation: the raised side goes toward the frame's front/down tube.

Evidence: Mike's Mini Motors Mega Moto build series (https://www.mikesminimotors.com/builds/mega-moto-mini-bike/) shows this exact plate being installed on a Mega Moto frame with a Predator 212 — confirming the plate works with this combination.

Why this setup:

  • Center exit routes the pipe rearward and under the seat frame — ideal for keeping hot pipes away from rider legs
  • The 3-stage design allows positioning the muffler outlet toward the rear
  • Adding the B91XL silencer reduces noise AND provides a heat-shielded outlet end cap, reducing burn risk for children
  • Mike's Mini Motors video "Mega Moto Mini Bike - RLV Muffler Install and Sound Comparison" confirms an RLV-style muffler was used on an active Mega Moto build

Routing Summary

The GPS-0020-CR center exit is the best fit for your requirements (rearward, under-seat). It exits from the engine, runs under the frame, and can be extended with a muffler pointing rearward past the seat post. This keeps all hot surfaces behind the rider and under the seat.

Fitment rating: Likely Fit — engine-level compatibility is confirmed (196/212). MM80 frame clearance is the strongest candidate among available headers but has not been 100% photo-verified with this exact plate/engine combo.

TASK 3 – Intake Selection

Recommended: SBK102-BLACK + KDPKSF (Stage 1 Intake Kit)

Part SKU Price Link Evidence

Air Filter 62mm, K&N Style Performance KDPKSF $11.95 GoPowerSports 4.9★; 62mm neck matches adapter

Total intake: $27.90 + shipping

Why this works:

  • The SBK102 adapter bolts directly to the stock Predator 212 carburetor (both hemi and non-hemi), replacing the bulky stock air box
  • It's only ~0.75" thick and angles the filter downward/rearward — low profile that clears frame rails
  • The KDPKSF 62mm conical filter is the standard companion pod filter for this adapter
  • Combined height is ~3-4" from the carb mouth — significantly lower than the stock plastic box
  • This is the standard "Stage 1" intake used on thousands of Predator 212 minibike builds

Fitment rating: Likely Fit — The adapter fits the Predator 212 carb with 4.9★ reviews confirming this. The low profile makes it very likely to clear the MM80 frame rails and stock fuel tank location. No interference reported on similar builds.

⚠ Note: Adding a pod filter leans the air/fuel mixture. You'll need to re-jet the carburetor. GoPowerSports sells jet kits.

TASK 6 – Risk Assessment

Component Risk Mitigation
MMFAMPBENT plate Bolt holes may need 1-2mm slotting for perfect alignment Round file or Dremel. Check Mike's Mini Motors Part 1 for the exact process.
GPS-0020-CR header Center exit may contact seat pan or rear tire Measure clearance before final tightening. Bend bracket or add spacer if needed.
SBK102 adapter Pod filter may contact frame rail on non-hemi engines (carb sits at different angle) Use a 45° or 90° carb adapter if clearance is tight (~$10 extra).
Fuel tank relocation Most common issue — stock tank WILL hit top frame tube Purchase a frame-mount tank. This is a required mod, not optional.
Chain alignment Clutch sprocket may not align perfectly with rear sprocket Adapter plate slotted holes allow fore-aft adjustment. Use a straightedge.
Throttle linkage Stock Predator 212 throttle linkage may not reach the handlebar GoPowerSports sells throttle linkage kits. Check Mike's Mini Motors build for their solution.

Parts Most Likely to Require Modification

  1. Frame bolt holes — may need slotting/drilling for adapter plate (low probability, medium effort)
  2. Seat pan — may need clearance notch for center-exit header (low-medium probability, easy)
  3. Fuel tank mount — requires bracket fabrication or purchase (certain — required mod)
  4. Exhaust hanger — may need a small bracket to secure muffler to frame (likely, easy)

Parts That Should NOT Need Modification

  • Predator 212 engine mounts to plate (slotted holes compensate)
  • Clutch mounts to crank (standard 3/4" bore)
  • Pod filter mounts to carb via adapter

TASK 7 – Assembly Instructions

Required Tools

  • Socket set (metric: 10mm, 12mm, 14mm; SAE: 3/8", 7/16", 1/2")
  • Torque wrench (ft-lb)
  • Hex/Allen key set
  • Round file or Dremel (for hole slotting if needed)
  • Straightedge (for chain alignment)
  • Chain breaker tool (if shortening chain)
  • Screwdrivers (flathead, Phillips)
  • Pliers
  • Wire cutters
  • Loctite 242 (blue) — critical for clutch bolt

Step 1: Remove Stock Engine

  1. Drain oil and fuel from stock 79cc engine
  2. Remove 4 mounting bolts holding stock engine to frame
  3. Lift stock engine out of frame
  4. Remove stock air box, stock muffler, and stock fuel tank
  5. Clean frame mounting area

Step 2: Prepare Adapter Plate (MMFAMPBENT)

  1. Inspect the plate — note which side is "bent" up (raised side goes toward the front/down-tube of the frame)
  2. Place plate on frame. Insert bolts through frame holes into plate holes
  3. Check alignment: If bolts don't slide in easily, mark the interference and use a round file or Dremel to open the hole 1-2mm
  4. If holes are significantly off, drill new holes in the plate using the frame as a template
  5. Install the 5/16" hardware: bolt → frame → plate → washer → nut (finger tight)

Step 3: Mount Engine to Plate

  1. Remove the 4 engine mounting bolts from the Predator 212
  2. Place Predator 212 on adapter plate
  3. Insert engine mounting bolts through engine feet → plate slotted holes → washers → nuts
  4. DO NOT TIGHTEN YET — leave loose for chain alignment
  5. Center the engine left-to-right on the plate

Step 4: Clutch Installation

  1. Clean the 3/4" crankshaft with brake cleaner
  2. Slide centrifugal clutch onto crankshaft — make sure key lines up with keyway
  3. Install the clutch retaining bolt with Loctite 242 (blue)
  4. Torque clutch bolt to spec (typically 15-20 ft-lb for small engines)

Step 5: Chain Alignment

  1. Place a straightedge against the rear sprocket and clutch sprocket
  2. Slide the engine forward/backward using the slotted plate holes until both sprockets are perfectly aligned
  3. Tighten the 4 engine mounting bolts and the 4 frame plate bolts
  4. Install the chain — should have ~1/2" of slack (about 10-15mm up/down movement in the middle of the chain)
  5. If chain is too long, use a chain breaker tool to remove links
  6. If chain is too short, purchase a longer chain (GoPowerSports or any motorcycle shop)

Step 6: Fuel Tank Relocation

  1. Verify stock tank hits frame — if using center-exit exhaust, the side-mount or frame-mount tank is better
  2. Options:
  3. GoPowerSports frame tank kit — bolts to the top frame tube ahead of the seat
  4. Universal flat tank — mount on the frame down-tube
  5. Rear rack tank — mount behind the seat
  6. Connect fuel line from new tank to carburetor

Step 7: Exhaust Installation

  1. Install GPS-0020-CR header on engine exhaust port
  2. Use a new exhaust gasket (included with some headers)
  3. Apply anti-seize to the studs
  4. Torque to spec (typically 15-20 ft-lb)
  5. Route the 3-stage center exit pipe rearward under the frame
  6. Check clearances:
  7. Seat pan — should clear by at least 1/2"
  8. Rear tire — should not contact at full suspension compression
  9. Clutch — pipe should not contact clutch or chain
  10. Install B91XL silencer at the pipe outlet
  11. Secure with RLV clamp (364144)
  12. Install exhaust hanger bracket (fabricate or purchase) to secure muffler to frame

Step 8: Intake Installation

  1. Remove stock air box from Predator 212 carburetor
  2. Install SBK102-BLACK adapter onto carburetor throat
  3. Tighten clamp screw
  4. Orient adapter so the filter points rearward or slightly down
  5. Install KDPKSF 62mm filter onto adapter
  6. Tighten clamp screw
  7. Verify clearance:
  8. Filter should not contact frame rails
  9. Filter should not contact fuel tank
  10. Filter should not contact the rear tire
  11. Install jet kit — install the recommended main jet for your altitude (typically #36-#38 for sea level with pod filter)

Step 9: Throttle Linkage

  1. Install throttle cable from handlebar to carburetor
  2. If stock Predator 212 cable is too short, purchase a minibike throttle cable kit from GoPowerSports
  3. Adjust cable tension at the carburetor — there should be a small amount of free play

Step 10: Final Clearance Checks

Before starting:

  • [ ] Adapter plate bolts tight
  • [ ] Engine mounting bolts tight
  • [ ] Clutch bolt tight (with Loctite 242)
  • [ ] Chain properly tensioned (1/2" slack)
  • [ ] Chain alignment verified (straightedge)
  • [ ] All exhaust bolts tight (with anti-seize)
  • [ ] Intake clamps tight
  • [ ] Fuel line connected and no leaks
  • [ ] Throttle operates smoothly (full open to full close)
  • [ ] Kill switch wired and functional
  • [ ] Brakes operational
  • [ ] No contact between exhaust and any frame/seat/tire component
  • [ ] No contact between intake/filter and any frame/seat/tire component

Rider safety check: - [ ] No exposed hot pipe where rider's calf can contact - [ ] Exhaust outlet points rearward, not at rider's leg - [ ] Fuel tank securely mounted - [ ] Kill switch accessible while riding

Step 11: First Start & Break-In

  1. Fill with fresh oil (SAE 10W-30, ~0.6 qt for Predator 212)
  2. Add fuel
  3. Choke on, throttle at idle
  4. Pull start — should start within 3-4 pulls
  5. Let idle for 30 seconds, then blip throttle
  6. Check for leaks: oil, fuel, exhaust
  7. First ride: 15-20 minutes at varying RPM — don't hold full throttle for extended periods
  8. After first heat cycle: Re-torque exhaust bolts (they will loosen as gasket seats)
  9. Change oil after first 5 hours of operation

Step 12: Long-Term Safety (Children Riders)

  • Install header wrap on any exposed exhaust pipe (reduces burn risk by 200-300°F surface temp)
  • Consider a clutch cover (GoPowerSports sells them for MM80) — keeps fingers and clothing out
  • Install a tether kill switch for extra safety
  • Heat shield the underside of the seat if exhaust routes under it
  • Verify the throttle governor is functional (stock Predator 212 has one) — this limits top speed
