Quantum Computing Public Company Comparison: IonQ, D-Wave, Rigetti

Date: April 28, 2026 Purpose: Investor-style synopsis that separates technology reality from business hype.

Executive Summary

The three publicly traded pure-play quantum computing companies — IonQ, D-Wave Quantum, and Rigetti Computing — finished FY2025 at very different positions. IonQ emerged as the clear financial leader ($130M revenue, 202% YoY growth, $3.3B cash, $370M RPO), driven by trapped-ion gate-model systems sold to commercial and government customers through all three major cloud platforms. D-Wave posted $24.6M revenue (179% YoY), but this was driven by annealing quantum systems — a fundamentally different (and narrower) computational model than gate-model quantum computing — and bookings actually fell 22% YoY to $18.7M before bouncing back in early 2026 with $32M+ in new bookings. Rigetti struggled: revenue declined 34% to $7.1M, the 108-qubit Cepheus-1 launch slipped, and government contract reauthorization paused. All three remain deeply unprofitable, sustained by large capital raises. The critical tension: D-Wave has paying customers using real technology today but on a model (quantum annealing) with limited applicability; IonQ has the highest technology ceiling (trapped ions, 99.99% gate fidelity, fault-tolerant roadmap) and strongest commercial traction but trades at a premium valuation and faces dilution from the $1.8B SkyWater acquisition; Rigetti has the weakest commercial traction but a credible superconducting roadmap if execution improves.

IonQ

1. Technology

Core approach: Trapped-ion gate-model quantum computing. Ions (typically ytterbium or barium) are confined in electromagnetic traps and manipulated with laser or microwave pulses. This provides all-to-all qubit connectivity — any qubit can interact directly with any other — a major architectural advantage over superconducting approaches.

Current technical maturity: Deployed Tempo development systems (~100 physical qubits) with 99.99% two-qubit gate fidelity (world record, Oct 2025). Published detailed fault-tolerant blueprint (Walking Cat Architecture, Apr 2026). Demonstrated photonic interconnection of two independent trapped-ion systems (Apr 2026). Targeting 12 logical qubits with error <1e-7 by end of 2026. Has shipped commercial systems (Forte Enterprise, Tempo) to paying customers.

Key strengths: - Highest gate fidelity in the industry — 99.99% 2Q gate fidelity means dramatically less overhead for error correction - All-to-all connectivity — no nearest-neighbor routing overhead; simplifies compilation for useful algorithms - Modular architecture — photonic interconnects enable scaling without sacrificing coherence; demonstrated real entanglement links between two systems - Long coherence times — trapped ions maintain quantum states orders of magnitude longer than superconducting qubits - Walking Cat Architecture provides a concrete, detailed engineering blueprint — not just a roadmap slide

Key weaknesses / bottlenecks: - Slow gate speeds — trapped-ion gates are microseconds vs nanoseconds for superconducting; limits clock speed for deep circuits - Laser complexity — systems require precision alignment of multiple laser beams; adds engineering complexity at scale - Cryogenics-free at room temp, but some setups still need vacuum chambers and integration complexity - Scaling beyond ~100 qubits in a single trap is physically challenging; modular approach is necessary but unproven at scale - Acquisition integration risk — absorbing Oxford Ionics, ID Quantique, Lightsynq, and now SkyWater simultaneously

Technology win case: Trapped ions deliver the fastest path to fault-tolerant quantum computing because the path to logical qubits requires far fewer physical qubits (thanks to high fidelity). The modular photonic-linking approach scales naturally to thousands of logical qubits. IonQ becomes the first company to demonstrate a useful error-corrected quantum computation.

Technology loss case: Slow gate speeds prove fundamentally limiting for deep quantum circuits. Photonic interconnects introduce too much noise or latency at scale. Superconducting systems catch up on fidelity and leap ahead on gate speed, making trapped ions a niche approach.

2. Business

Strategy and target markets: Full-stack vertically integrated quantum computing company. Selling quantum computing as a service (QCaaS) via cloud platforms plus on-premises enterprise systems. Target markets: life sciences, financial services, manufacturing, defense, logistics. The SkyWater acquisition (pending) would add domestic semiconductor fab capability, making IonQ the first vertically integrated quantum company that can fab its own control chips.

Revenue model: - Cloud access fees (AWS Braket, Azure Quantum, GCP Marketplace) - On-premises system sales (Forte Enterprise, Tempo) - Managed services / capacity reservations - Government contracts (defense, energy, research)

Major customers, partnerships, government contracts: - Cloud: AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud — only quantum company on all three major platforms - Enterprise: QuantumBasel ($60M+ multi-year partnership, extended to 2029), Einride (logistics), CCRM (healthcare) - Government: DARPA HARQ contract (Apr 2026); Missile Defense Agency SHIELD IDIQ ($151B potential ceiling, individually competed task orders); launched IonQ Federal (dedicated government division); $100M+ in existing government contracts; DOE MoU for space-based quantum tech - Academic: University of Chicago (IonQ Center for Engineering and Science) - Recent acquisitions: Oxford Ionics (2D ion trap, microwave control), Lightsynq (photonic interconnects), ID Quantique (quantum networking), SkyWater Tech ($1.8B pending) - Note: A February 2026 short-seller report alleged loss of significant Pentagon contracts; IonQ has disputed this.

Business strengths: - First quantum company to surpass $100M annual GAAP revenue - Commercial revenue >60% of mix (not just grants/research) - International revenue >30% (geographic diversification) - $370M RPO provides meaningful forward visibility - $3.3B cash war chest enables aggressive M&A and R&D - Triple-cloud distribution lowers customer acquisition friction

D-Wave Quantum

1. Technology

Core approach: Quantum annealing (primary) and gate-model quantum computing (new, accelerated roadmap). Quantum annealing finds the minimum-energy state of a problem Hamiltonian — well-suited for optimization, but not for general-purpose quantum computation. D-Wave's Advantage2 annealing processor has ~4,800+ qubits. The company is now pursuing a dual-platform strategy after acquiring Quantum Circuits Inc. (QCI) in early 2026 for $550M, aiming to ship an initial gate-model system in 2026.

Current technical maturity: Annealing technology is commercially deployed — sixth-generation systems (Advantage2) with paying customers running real workloads. Claimed a quantum supremacy milestone in early 2025 for magnetic materials simulation. However, the claims are contested — critics argue annealing systems solve a fundamentally different (and narrower) class of problems than universal gate-model QC. The gate-model effort is early-stage, accelerated by the QCI acquisition.

Key strengths: - Only company with commercial quantum computers generating real customer revenue — annealing technology actually works for specific optimization problems - 4,800+ qubits — far more than any gate-model competitor; qubit count is partially misleading (annealing qubits are not comparable to gate-model qubits) but impressive for its domain - Hybrid quantum-classical solvers — practical approach combining QPU + classical HPC; customers can integrate ML models into optimization workflows (introduced Qubits 2026) - Multi-chip processor fabric — path to further scaling annealing processors - QCI acquisition provides gate-model IP — dual-rail superconducting technology claims speed of superconducting + fidelity of trapped ions

Key weaknesses / bottlenecks: - Quantum annealing is NOT universal quantum computing — it solves optimization problems and simulation of certain physical systems, not Shor's algorithm, Grover's search, or general quantum circuits - Contested quantum advantage — competitors argue classical heuristics or specialized hardware can match or beat D-Wave on most optimization benchmarks - Gate-model effort is unproven — QCI acquisition accelerates the roadmap but the technology hasn't been commercially deployed yet - Annealing market may be smaller than gate-model market — optimization problems are everywhere, but many can be solved adequately with classical methods - Bookings declined 22% in FY2025 — excluding one large system sale, underlying commercial momentum is less clear

Technology win case: Quantum annealing proves irreplaceable for a class of high-value optimization problems (logistics, materials science, financial portfolio optimization). D-Wave's hybrid solvers become the standard for quantum-augmented HPC. QCI's gate-model technology delivers a competitive superconducting gate-model system, giving D-Wave a dual-platform moat.

Technology loss case: Classical optimization heuristics and specialized hardware (GPUs, ASICs) continue to improve, eroding the value proposition of annealing. Gate-model competitors leapfrog D-Wave's delayed entry. The dual-platform strategy splits resources without achieving leadership in either approach.

Rigetti Computing

1. Technology

Core approach: Superconducting gate-model quantum computing. Qubits are tiny superconducting circuits (transmons) on a silicon chip, operated at millikelvin temperatures in dilution refrigerators. Chiplet-based architecture (multi-chip modules) for scaling. Fabricates its own chips at a dedicated fab in Fremont, CA.

Current technical maturity: Deployed 108-qubit Cepheus-1-108Q system (Q1 2026) with 99.1% median two-qubit gate fidelity. Ankaa-3 (84 qubits, 99.5% 2Q fidelity) launched Dec 2024. Cepheus-1-108Q launch was delayed — originally expected in 2025, pushed to Q1 2026. Roadmap targets 150+ qubits / 99.7% fidelity by end of 2026, and 1,000+ qubits / 99.8% fidelity by end of 2027.

Key strengths: - Foundry ownership — Rigetti is the only quantum computing company that operates its own semiconductor fab; provides vertical control over chip fabrication and potential foundry-as-a-service business - Chiplet-based modular architecture — Cepheus-1-108Q uses 12 interconnected 9-qubit chiplets; this is a plausible scaling path - Fast gate speeds — ~60ns 2Q gates (vs microseconds for trapped ions) - AWS Braket integration — established cloud presence - Quanta Computer partnership — $200M+ combined investment over 5 years for manufacturing scale-up; $35M stock investment from Quanta

Key weaknesses / bottlenecks: - Lowest gate fidelity among the three — 99.1% vs IonQ's 99.99%; means vastly more error-correction overhead - Delayed Cepheus-1-108Q — timeline slip erodes investor confidence; originally promised 100+ qubits "by end of 2025" - Revenue declining — 34% drop in FY2025 is hard to explain as "transition" - Dilution is severe — shares outstanding grew at extreme rates, and insider selling was flagged by analysts - 1000+ qubit by 2027 roadmap is extremely aggressive — no one has demonstrated that superconducting scaling to 1000+ qubits at 99.8% fidelity is achievable at this pace

Technology win case: Chiplet architecture scales faster than monolithic approaches. Rigetti reaches 1000+ qubits by 2027 with 99.8%+ fidelity, leapfrogging trapped-ion competitors on qubit count and gate speed. 99.9% 2Q gate fidelity enables error correction and fault-tolerant operations. Tight fab integration reduces cost per qubit.

Technology loss case: Fidelity improvement plateaus — 99.8%+ becomes increasingly difficult. Chiplet interconnects introduce noise that limits scaling. Trapped-ion or other approaches deliver fault-tolerant logical qubits first. Delays continue to push the roadmap right.

Strategy and target markets: Full-stack superconducting quantum computing — fabricates its own chips, operates cloud services, sells on-premises systems. Targets research institutions, government labs, and enterprise adopters. Exploring "foundry-as-a-service" business model — selling chip fabrication to other quantum companies.

Revenue model: - Rigetti Quantum Cloud Services (QCS) — tiered subscription access - On-premises system sales (Novera QPU, Cepheus systems) - Foundry services (theoretical, early stage) - Government and research contracts

Summary Judgments

1. Strongest Technology Position: IonQ

IonQ has the highest gate fidelity (99.99% 2Q), the most credible fault-tolerant roadmap (Walking Cat Architecture), demonstrated all-to-all connectivity, and real photonic interconnect between systems. The trapped-ion approach fundamentally requires fewer physical qubits per logical qubit because fidelity is higher — meaning a useful fault-tolerant quantum computer may arrive sooner even with lower absolute qubit counts. D-Wave's annealing technology works for its domain but is not universal quantum computing. Rigetti's superconducting approach is the industry standard but IonQ has lapped them on fidelity.

2. Strongest Business Position: IonQ

$130M revenue (202% growth), $3.3B cash, $370M RPO, triple-cloud distribution, 60%+ commercial revenue mix, and a government division (IonQ Federal) with substantial contract exposure. D-Wave is a distant second ($24.6M revenue, $884M cash, 135+ customers but lower revenue quality). Rigetti's $7.1M declining revenue makes it a clear third.

3. Highest Risk / Reward: D-Wave Quantum

D-Wave offers the most contrarian bet: they have real commercial revenue from annealing today, but the gate-model quantum computing narrative belongs to IonQ and competitors. If the QCI acquisition delivers a competitive gate-model system and annealing proves irreplaceable for optimization, D-Wave's dual-platform strategy could create an asymmetric outcome. The risk: annealing turns out to be a niche with a small TAM, and gate-model entry comes too late.

IonQ's risk/reward is more moderate — the financial position is strong enough that bankruptcy is not a real risk, but the valuation already prices in significant success. SkyWater acquisition and dilution are real but manageable.

Rigetti's risk/reward is the most skewed toward downside right now given the revenue decline, delays, and dilution. A turnaround (1000-qubit system on time, C-DAC success) would produce spectacular returns — but the signal quality from early 2026 is poor.

4. Weakest Overall Thesis: Rigetti Computing

Three data points drive this: revenue declined 34% in FY2025 to $7.1M (smallest base, falling). The Cepheus-1-108Q was delayed. Insider sales and heavy dilution erode shareholder alignment. The 1000+ qubit / 99.8% fidelity roadmap by 2027 is the most aggressive in the industry with the least evidence that it's achievable. IonQ already has 99.99%. D-Wave already has commercial revenue and growth. Rigetti has a credible technology approach (superconducting chiplet architecture) but execution has been poor, and the financial trajectory raises existential questions about whether they can fund the roadmap without further extreme dilution.

Rigetti's only plausible recovery scenario requires: (1) Cepheus-1-108Q fidelity improvements on schedule, (2) C-DAC and Novera revenue materializing in 2026, (3) the 1000-qubit 2027 target being met without major delays, and (4) enterprise cloud subscriptions growing meaningfully. That's four conditional statements with poor track records.

Broader Competitive Context

The three public pure-plays do not compete in a vacuum. IBM, Google Quantum AI, and Quantinuum represent significant incumbent and private-sector competition — each with deeper pockets, longer runways, or existing enterprise relationships. This section situates IonQ, D-Wave, and Rigetti within the broader competitive landscape.

IBM Quantum

IBM operates the longest-running corporate quantum program (public since 2016). As of early 2026: - The IBM Quantum Network has grown to 250+ enterprise, academic, and research organizations, making it the largest quantum ecosystem by member count. This includes Fortune 500 companies (Booz Allen, Goldman Sachs, ExxonMobil, CERN, Samsung, Mitsubishi, Wells Fargo, JPMorgan) and 60+ startups. - IBM's Condor 2 processor (1,121 superconducting qubits, launched 2024) was the first utility-scale quantum processor with >1,000 qubits. IBM is now developing Flintlock (1,386 qubits) and planning Flintlock+ (1,600+ qubits) for 2026, with a roadmap toward Blue Jay (1,932 qubits, 2028) and Starling (mK-scale, ~16K qubits, 2030+). - The Qiskit ecosystem remains the most widely used quantum software development kit globally. Qiskit Patterns (commercial) provides pre-built quantum circuits and integration templates for specific industry workflows — an attempt to lower the barrier for enterprise adoption. - IBM's error correction roadmap targets 100 logical qubits with error suppression by 2029, using the gross code. Heavy-hex lattice architecture has shown 99.8%+ 2Q fidelity on Heron processors. - How they compare: IBM's advantage is scale (largest ecosystem, biggest QPU count) and enterprise distribution (IBM Cloud, decades of enterprise relationships). Its weakness: superconducting fidelity still trails IonQ's trapped-ion record (99.99% vs ~99.85%), and IBM's qubits require heavy error-correction overhead. IBM is not a public pure-play so there is no stock-per-stock comparison, but as a technology competitor, IBM's Condor/Flintlock roadmap is directly competitive with Rigetti's superconducting trajectory. If IBM reaches 1,600+ qubits with 99.9%+ fidelity by 2027, Rigetti's 1,000-qubit target looks less differentiated. For IonQ, trapped-ion vs superconducting is a fundamental architectural competition — IBM's scale advantages in superconducting matter less if trapped ions reach fault-tolerance first.

Google Quantum AI

Google maintains one of the world's most advanced quantum programs despite not being a public pure-play. Key developments: - Willow processor (105 superconducting qubits, announced December 2024) was a landmark achievement: Google demonstrated that adding more physical qubits reduced error rates below the surface code threshold — an exponential suppression of errors that had never been experimentally shown before. This is arguably the most important single quantum computing result in the last 2-3 years. - Google's roadmap targets 1000+ logical qubits by 2030, with intermediate milestones: demonstrating a useful error-corrected quantum computation ("beyond classical") on a real-world problem by 2027-2028. - Google has an estimated $1B+ annual quantum R&D budget (not publicly disclosed, but inferred from headcount, lab infrastructure, and published research output). This exceeds the combined R&D spend of IonQ, D-Wave, and Rigetti. - Google collaborates with academic institutions (error correction theory, materials simulation) and offers Google Quantum Virtual Lab (cloud-based quantum simulation for enterprise partners). - How they compare: Google is the single most important technological competitor to the entire pure-play sector. With a $1B+ R&D budget and deep engineering talent, Google can pursue a long-duration, capital-intensive research program without market pressure. The Willow result directly challenges IonQ's narrative that trapped-ions are the only path to fault-tolerance — if superconducting error correction works at Google's scale, the architectural race narrows. Google's timeline (useful error-corrected computation by 2027-2028) is roughly on par with IonQ's logical qubit roadmap. The asymmetry: Google does not need to generate quantum revenue — it can fund the program indefinitely from its core business, whereas pure-plays must eventually monetize. However, Google's quantum program is a cost center, not a P&L driver, so it faces less urgency and less focus than a dedicated public company. Google has no plans to spin off Quantum AI as a separate public entity, meaning investors cannot directly capture its quantum upside.

Quantinuum (Honeywell-backed)

Quantinuum (formed 2021 via merger of Honeywell Quantum Solutions and Cambridge Quantum) is the leading private trapped-ion competitor to IonQ. Key facts: - H2 processor (64 physical qubits, 99.99%+ 2Q gate fidelity) — essentially tied with IonQ on fidelity. Quantinuum claims the highest quantum volume in the industry and has demonstrated real-time error correction (mid-circuit measurement, feed-forward). - Strong intellectual property portfolio: Quantinuum holds 400+ quantum computing patents across trapped-ion hardware, quantum software (TKET compiler), quantum cybersecurity (Quantum Origin — quantum random number generation + quantum key distribution), and quantum chemistry (InQuanto). - Revenue model: QCaaS via direct access and cloud platforms (Azure Quantum, AWS Braket); quantum cybersecurity products (Quantum Origin deployed at multiple financial institutions); quantum chemistry software licensing. Revenue is not public but estimated at $50-100M for FY2025 (below IonQ but above D-Wave). - Honeywell backing: Honeywell (parent, ~$40B revenue) provides financial stability. Quantinuum raised $300M in a Series B (2024) at a $5B valuation and has been reportedly preparing for an IPO (possibly 2026-2027 in London or US). - Dual HQ: Cambridge (UK) and Broomfield (Colorado); strong government relationships (UK National Quantum Strategy, US federal contracts). - How they compare: Quantinuum is the single most formidable competitive threat to IonQ. Both use trapped-ion technology with identical fidelity (99.99% 2Q). Quantinuum has a lead in quantum volume and demonstrated real-time error correction. IonQ has advantages in top-line revenue ($130M vs est. $50-100M), triple-cloud distribution, $3.3B cash, and a more aggressive M&A approach. Quantinuum has Honeywell's patience and deep pockets — it does not face the same dilution pressure or short-term revenue expectations. Quantinuum is the most likely candidate to disrupt IonQ's pure-play trapped-ion monopoly. If Quantinuum IPOs in 2026-2027, it would become a direct fourth public quantum stock and potentially the most serious competitive threat.

Cross-cutting scenario observations:

  • Regulation & national security: All three benefit from the US government's drive for domestic quantum computing capability. China/Europe/Japan quantum programs also create export opportunities. But government dependency also creates concentration risk.
  • Fault-tolerant threshold: The single most important binary event in the industry over the next 5 years is the first demonstration of a useful error-corrected quantum computation. Whoever achieves this first (IonQ, Google, Quantinuum, IBM, or another player) will define the competitive landscape for a decade. The rest may be relegated to niche or acquisition targets.
  • Consolidation risk: Given the number of well-funded private quantum companies (Quantinuum, PsiQuantum, Alice & Bob, Xanadu, EeroQ) and public pure-plays, industry consolidation is likely. M&A could be a bull case catalyst for acquirers or a bear case outcome (acquisition at a discount) for targets.
  • Cash = optionality: All three public companies have at least 5-7 years of cash runway (even Rigetti at current burn rate). A severe bear case requires either accelerated spending or complete loss of revenue, not just delayed growth.
  • Dilution arithmetic: Current holders in all three should expect continued dilution. Even in bull cases, the number of shares outstanding may grow 50-100% over 5 years, meaning per-share returns lag absolute enterprise value creation. Investors should model per-share returns, not EV returns.
