Executive Summary

SpaceX completed the largest IPO in history on June 12, 2026 at $135/share, raising ~$75B. In its first four trading sessions, the stock surged 70%+, reaching a market capitalization of approximately $2.6–$2.75 trillion as of June 16 — vaulting past Amazon ($2.4T) to become the 5th most valuable company globally.

This is a valuation without historical precedent relative to current financial fundamentals. The critical question is not merely whether SpaceX is overvalued, but whether the magnitude of overvaluation has any historical parallel.

4. Reverse DCF Analysis

"How Much Success Is Already Priced In?"

To justify a $2.65T market cap today (DCF: 12% WACC, 3% terminal growth, 15-year horizon):

Scenario A (40% margins by 2030):

  • Requires 2035 revenue of ~$400B (19x current)
  • Requires 2030 revenue of ~$180B (9x current)
  • Implies ~50% CAGR through 2030

Scenario B (30% margins by 2030):

  • Requires 2035 revenue of ~$550B (27x current)
  • Requires 2030 revenue of ~$250B (12x current)

Scenario C (20% margins):

  • Requires 2035 revenue of ~$800B (39x current)
  • Requires 2030 revenue of ~$400B (19x current)

What $250B revenue by 2030 looks like:

  • Starlink: 50M subscribers × $100/mo = $60B/yr
  • Starship cargo: 500 metric tons/month = $15B
  • Launch: 500 launches/yr × $50M = $25B
  • Government/Starshield: $20B
  • xAI/AI: $80B (rivaling Google Cloud today)
  • Point-to-point, Moon/Mars, other: $50B
This would require SpaceX to be larger than today's Amazon ($620B rev) by 2030, with the entire global launch market growing 20x from today's levels.

To generate $200B+ in annual FCF by 2035 (implied by $2.65T): Need $400B+ revenue with 50%+ EBITDA margins — making SpaceX the most profitable company in human history by a wide margin.

The market is pricing in: 10-20x revenue growth in 5 years; Starlink near-total global broadband dominance; Starship achieving 90%+ cost reduction AND >80% market share; AI acquisitions becoming the next Google/Amazon; no meaningful competition; capex receding dramatically.

"How much success is already priced in?" — "Everything that could possibly go right, does go right, and on an accelerated timeline."

7. Bull Thesis — The Case for $2.65T

7.1 The Multiples Argument Is Misguided

SpaceX is not a "rocket company." It is: a next-gen infrastructure monopolist (Starlink), an industrial platform (only fully reusable rocket system), a defense prime (Starshield), an AI player (xAI + Cursor), and a long-duration growth option (Starship → Moon → Mars). Trailing multiples are irrelevant for a company growing 58%+ annually with trillions in addressable market.

7.2 Starlink Economics Approach Monopoly

  • 7M+ subscribers growing at ~60% YoY
  • High switching costs: proprietary hardware ($599-2,500)
  • Only operational LEO broadband constellation with global coverage
  • Capacity to add 2-3M subscribers per year
  • Musk stated $30-50B annual revenue by 2028
  • On track for segment-level profitability in 2026
  • V2 satellites with direct-to-cell open carrier wholesale revenue

7.3 Starship Is Transformational

  • Reduces cost to orbit from ~$1,500/kg (Falcon 9) to ~$100/kg or less
  • 100+ tons to orbit vs Falcon 9's 22 tons
  • If airline-like operations ($10M/launch): could open in-space manufacturing, orbital refueling, large-scale space stations, lunar cargo, asteroid extraction
  • Military applications (rapid global cargo, space-based platforms) strategically vital

7.4 The Musk Premium Is Real

  • Musk has repeatedly defied skeptics on timelines and feasibility
  • 2x-4x cost advantage through vertical integration
  • 10x faster development than incumbents
  • Ability to attract/retain top engineering talent unmatched
  • xAI/Cursor acquisitions give legitimate AI capability

7.5 Valuation Could Be Justified Over Time

  • Starlink: 50M subs × $120/mo = $72B rev, 50% margins = $36B EBITDA
  • Starship: 500 launches/yr × $50M = $25B rev, 30% margins = $7.5B EBITDA
  • Starshield/Defense: $15B rev, 40% margins = $6B EBITDA
  • xAI/Grok: $40B rev, 30% margins = $12B EBITDA
  • Point-to-point Earth: $10-20B rev by 2035

Total EBITDA by 2032-2035: $60-80B. At 30-35x EBITDA: $1.8-2.8 trillion.

The bull case is that by ~2032-2035, SpaceX organically grows into its valuation — the stock is flat for 6-9 years while financials catch up.

7.6 Why Current Investors Might Be Right

  • IPO massively oversubscribed — deep institutional demand
  • 20%+ daily gains — large buyers cannot accumulate fast enough
  • Tiny float (~10-15%) — price discovery unreliable until more shares unlock
  • Sovereign wealth funds and long-duration pension capital are natural buyers of scarce, hard-to-replicate assets
  • If Starlink reaches $40B revenue by 2028 at 40% margins, 40x EBITDA yields $1.6T — plausible within 2 years

8. Final Verdict

Is SpaceX Overvalued?

If you believe SpaceX is an innovative aerospace/defense company that will continue to grow rapidly but face competition and capital constraints: YES, spectacularly overvalued. By every conventional measure, the current price is indefensible.

If you believe SpaceX is the foundational infrastructure company of the 21st century — controlling access to orbit, operating the backbone of global connectivity, and sitting on a transformational platform (Starship) that could multiply the in-space economy by 100x: The valuation is aggressive but potentially sustainable.

The Hardest Question

"Is SpaceX actually overvalued, or does it merely feel overvalued because the valuation is difficult for traditional investors to comprehend?"

The honest answer: Both are true simultaneously.

  1. It feels overvalued because it is — The magnitude of multiples is historically unprecedented. Even at Tesla's 2021 peak, P/S was 23x, not 128x. Even Cisco at the dot-com peak was 29x sales — and Cisco was profitable.

  2. It feels overvalued because it's hard to comprehend — SpaceX is genuinely building capabilities with no historical precedent. Reusable rockets that land themselves. A satellite constellation covering the entire planet. A rocket larger than Saturn V that can fly daily. These are not incremental innovations.

  3. The valuation is being pulled into the future by enormous structural demand — More buyers than sellers, tiny float. This is the single most important factor in the current price. It is not an efficient market for SpaceX stock right now.

  4. "This time is different" has been the most expensive phrase in investing history — The magnitude of the premium ($2.65T for a company that just lost $5B on $20B revenue) is so extreme that even if SpaceX achieves everything Musk promises, a long-term investor buying today will likely earn poor returns.

Recommendation

For a short position: The structural case for mean reversion is as strong as any in market history. However, the tiny float, massive institutional demand, and possibility of continued momentum mean timing is extremely difficult. A short position requires patience for lock-up expiry, tolerance for 50-100% continued upside, and careful position sizing. The risk/reward is eventually compelling but near-term pain is almost certain.

For a long position: Buying at 128x sales with negative earnings requires extraordinary conviction that SpaceX will become the most successful company in human history by a wide margin. Even then, returns are likely to be disappointing for a decade. The risk/reward is unfavorable.

Most honest assessment: SpaceX is a genuinely historic company with transformative technology. Its stock price has disconnected from fundamental reality. The gap between its current price and any reasonable DCF-based fair value is larger than any comparable gap in the history of large-cap equities. This does not mean the price will fall tomorrow — but it does mean that buying at these levels requires accepting a level of valuation risk that has no historical parallel.
