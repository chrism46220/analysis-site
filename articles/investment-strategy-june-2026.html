Compiled: June 4, 2026 Sources: Macro outlook (Apr 26), AI bubble report (May 21), quantum analysis (Apr 28), oil crisis comparison (Apr 28), portfolio analysis (Apr 26), Thomas multi-source research (Apr 26), Plato growth stock reports (Apr 26), Plato classification framework, Plato distillation on Thomas Asset base: Existing positions in TSM (+33%), WMT (+34%), BROS (−12.6%)

1. Macro Context — The Governing Reality

Three forces pin the market:

A. Fed paralysis. Rate at 3.64%, cut ~70bps from peak. CPI reaccelerated to 3.29% — cannot cut aggressively. GDP approaching stall — cannot tighten. The Fed is pinned. This limits the upside for rate-sensitive equities and compresses multiple-expansion potential. Yield curve (2yr 3.83% / 10yr 4.34%) is uninverted, the traditional recession signal has triggered.

B. AI capex/revenue arithmetic. $660–690B in 2026 AI infrastructure capex vs ~$37B in enterprise AI revenue. The math does not work without massive acceleration. P(Bubble) = 57% leaning yes, per AI bubble report (May 21). But mega-caps (NVDA, MSFT, AMZN, META, GOOGL) have real earnings to absorb the overhang. The bubble risk is concentrated in mid-cap AI infrastructure (APLD, NBIS) and unprofitable AI startups — not in the hyperscalers themselves.

C. Mild recession most likely scenario. Tariff drag + fading fiscal impulse + consumer exhaustion → 1–2 quarters negative GDP. Not a credit event — no housing leg. Expect GDP prints −0.5% to −1.0%, earnings drop 5–10%, unemployment uptick 25–50bps. Market typically bottoms 4 months before recession ends. Calendar: trough Q3–Q4 2026, recovery H1 2027.

Quantum context: IonQ $130M revenue, D-Wave $24.6M, Rigetti $7.1M. Timeline analog = AI in 1998–2004 (pre-AlexNet). Commercially transformative: 2031–2035. Pure lottery tickets at current market caps.

Oil shock overlay: Structural shift to renewables/EVs/storage. Legacy ICE/airlines/energy-intensive manufacturing underperform. Tariffs accelerate re-shoring = grid tech, industrial efficiency, domestic energy gain.

2. Asset Allocation Framework

Base case: 60/25/15 (equities/bonds/cash) — defensively positioned for mild recession.

Scenario Probability Equities Bonds Cash
Mild recession (base) 50% 60% 25% 15%
Soft landing / no recession 25% 75% 15% 10%
Hard recession / credit event 15% 40% 35% 25%
Stagflation (persistent 3%+ CPI + negative GDP) 10% 35% 20% 45%

Rationale: CPI at 3.29% means nominal bonds offer negative real yield at current rates. But treasuries still provide recession hedging and optionality for re-entry. Cash at 15% is dry powder for the recession trough. If hard recession materializes (credit spreads blow out, HY OAS > 600bps), shift to 40/35/25.

Bond selection: Short-to-intermediate duration (2–7yr). Avoid long-duration in reacceleration risk. TIPS allocation (30% of bond sleeve) for CPI hedge. IG corporate only — no high-yield until recession is priced in.

5. Risk Management

Position Sizing Rules

  1. No single position > 12% of equity sleeve (NVDA is the only exception for conviction).
  2. AI infrastructure aggregate cap: 65% of equity (conviction 45% + watchlist 10% + data center 8% + quantum 2%). Do not exceed.

  4. Cash floor: Never below 8% of total portfolio unless recession is confirmed over.
  5. Each purchase tranche: Minimum 3 tranches per position (1/3 initial, 1/3 on pullback, 1/3 at aggressive buy).

7. Honest Caveats

Macro Uncertainty

  • Fed is pinned. CPI at 3.29% limits cuts; GDP near stall limits hikes. If CPI reaccelerates toward 4% — stagflation scenario — this entire strategy needs revision. Duration would be wrong, growth stocks would compress, and the 60/25/15 split would be too aggressive.
  • Recession probability is elevated but uncertain. The mild recession scenario (50% probability) is the base case, but we haven't had a real recession since 2020 (Covid was exogenous, not structural). Market behavior in a tariff-driven slowdown is poorly modeled.
  • The oil crisis comparison is structural, not cyclical. The energy transition thesis holds over 5+ years, but 1–2 quarters of oil price volatility from tariff disruption could create false signals. Do not over-allocate on daily oil headlines.

AI Capex ROI — The 57% Bubble Problem

  • $660–690B capex vs $37B enterprise AI revenue is the single most alarming statistic in the research base. The gap is over 15x. Something has to give — either revenue accelerates dramatically (possible but unproven) or capex pulls back (triggering a correction in ALL AI positions).
  • The conviction list (NVDA, AVGO, AMZN, MSFT, ASML, AMD, MU) would drop 20–40% in an AI correction. There is no position-level hedge for this. The hedge is cash and bonds — not puts (too expensive in VIX regimes).
  • AI revenue acceleration might not materialize. Enterprise adoption is real (88% of organizations using AI per McKinsey), but monetization is unproven. If AI stays a cost center rather than a profit center, the capex cycle turns negative.
  • DeepSeek/open-source disruption risk. Open-weight models are narrowing the gap with proprietary models. If model commoditization happens faster than expected, moats compress for all AI infrastructure plays. NVDA's CUDA moat is the strongest defense; the rest are more vulnerable.

Quantum — Honest Probabilities

  • IONQ at $15–20B market cap on $130M revenue = 115–150x revenue. For comparison, NVDA at the peak of the GPU boom trades at ~25x revenue. Quantum multiples are pricing in decades of success.
  • Timeline: 10–15 years to commercial transformation. The AI 1998–2004 analog is useful for understanding the stage, but AI's transition from 1998 → 2012 (14 years) was as long as many investors' entire careers. Most call options will expire worthless.
  • Probabilistic assessment: 10% chance any pure-play quantum company returns 50x+ within 10 years. 20% chance they survive to 2032 as going concerns. 70% chance they're acquired, diluted, or bankrupt before commercial scale. Position at 2% maximum.
  • Google/Quantinuum risk: Google doesn't need public quantum revenue; it can develop fault-tolerant systems at a loss for years. If Google ships first, IonQ's narrative collapses. Quantinuum IPO (2026–2027) could be the most serious competitive threat.

  • All source reports are from April–May 2026. Market conditions can shift rapidly. The macro dashboard captures April 26 data — CPI, GDP, and Fed funds are now 5 weeks old. Re-verify before acting.
