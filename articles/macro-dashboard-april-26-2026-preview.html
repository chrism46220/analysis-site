5. Yield Curve (2yr vs 10yr Treasury)

Interpretation: The yield curve normalization is a welcome technical improvement, but historically, un-inversions have often preceded recessions within 6–18 months. The signal is ambiguous: technically healthier, but contextually a lagging recession warning.

Interpretation: The labor market is clearly cooling. Not in collapse, but the trajectory (rising unemployment, slowing hires) is textbook late-cycle softening.

Synthesis

1. Are consumers getting stronger or weaker? Why?

Weaker. Real spending is still positive, but the labor market support beneath it is eroding — unemployment is up, job creation is near stall speed, and CPI reaccelerated to 3.29% in March, eroding real purchasing power. Consumer credit growth is moderate, not surging, which means households aren’t levering up to sustain spending. This is a consumer being squeezed from two sides: less labor income growth + higher inflation.

2. Is the Federal Reserve likely to be constrained or flexible? Why?

Constrained. The Fed has cut to 3.64%, but the March CPI reacceleration to 3.29% dramatically limits further easing. The Fed cannot cut aggressively into rising inflation without risking a 1970s-style unanchoring. At the same time, GDP near stall and a softening labor market argue for accommodation. This is a stagflationary bind: the Fed is pinned — unable to ease enough to support growth, unable to tighten enough to crush inflation.

3. System condition: Stable, Building Risk, or Late Cycle?

Late Cycle, Building Risk.

Classic late-cycle signals present: - Yield curve un-inversion (historically recession-prone signal) - Unemployment rising from cycle lows (Sahm Rule indicator triggered) - Inflation reaccelerating - GDP growth decelerating sharply - Labor market softening meaningfully - Fed constrained — limited policy ammunition

The system is not in crisis, but the trajectory across multiple indicators is deteriorating simultaneously, which is the signature of a late-cycle transition.

4. What 1–2 indicators concern you the most right now?

  1. CPI (3.29% YoY, reaccelerating) — Most dangerous because it constrains the Fed. If inflation had stayed at 2.3%, the Fed could cut to support the weakening economy. Instead, they face stagflation risk.

  2. Real GDP (near-stall in Q4 2025) — Growth that was fine through mid-2025 has decelerated abruptly. A stall combined with reaccelerating inflation is the worst combination for policymakers.

These two together — slowing growth + rising inflation — define the stagflation risk that makes this macro environment genuinely uncomfortable, not just cyclically weak.

Plato’s Take

Three Buckets That Would Invalidate the Downturn Bias

If these thresholds are met, the assessment flips from “Late Cycle, Building Risk” → “Stable / Soft Landing Confirmed”:

1. Inflation: Sustained Sub-3% Core PCE

  • Current thesis risk: Inflation reacceleration (sticky services, tariff pass-through).
  • Invalidation trigger: 3 consecutive prints of Core PCE ≤ 2.8% (i.e., clearly trending toward 2%, not bouncing at 3.0–3.5%).
  • Why: The whole “Building Risk” call rests on the Fed being unable to cut. If inflation is sustainably falling, the Fed gets a rate-cut option, which relieves the primary pressure valve on the economy.
  • The real threshold: Watch Core PCE ex-housing — if that prints ≤ 2.5%, the services-inflation thesis is broken.

2. Labor Market: Stable Hiring Without Wage Spiral

  • Current thesis risk: NFP sliding toward ≤100K/month (softening) or ≥300K/month (overheating wages).
  • Invalidation trigger: 3-month average NFP of 150K–225K combined with AHE (avg hourly earnings) ≤ 3.9% YoY.
  • Why: The “Goldilocks” labor market — steady hiring that absorbs new entrants without stoking wage-driven services inflation. If AHE drops below 4% while NFP stays above 150K, the wage-price spiral risk is off the table.
  • The unemployment rate must stay ≤ 4.2%. A move to 4.3%+ with weak payrolls would confirm the downturn, not invalidate it.

3. Yield Curve: Normalization with Positive Slope

  • Current thesis risk: 2s10s remains inverted (or barely positive), signaling imminent recession.
  • Invalidation trigger: 2s10s spread holds at +50 bps or higher for 2+ months, with the curve maintaining positive slope even after a Fed cut.
  • Why: Inversion is the single most reliable recession signal. A durable steepening to +50bps+ would break the inversion-recession signaling chain. This matters more than the absolute level of rates.
  • Additional confirmation: 3m10s spread (more reliable than 2s10s for recession) must also be positive for 90+ consecutive days.

Tipping Point Combinations (Highest Conviction)

The downturn bias is not broken by one good print. It’s broken by a sustained vector shift:

Metric Must Show For Duration
Core PCE ≤ 2.8% 3 months
NFP avg 150K–225K 3 months
AHE YoY ≤ 3.9% 3 months
2s10s spread ≥ +50 bps 2 months
Unemployment ≤ 4.2% 3 months

If 4 of 6 meet threshold, flip to “Stable.” If all 6 meet threshold, flip to “Expansion Confirmed.”

What Would NOT Invalidate the Bias

  • One month of low CPI (volatile; could reverse)
  • A single Fed cut (could be panic cut)
  • Yield curve briefly steepening on recession fears (steepening inversion)
  • Stock market rally (equities can rally into recession)

Bottom Line

The current assessment is defensible until proven otherwise by sustained data, not one-off prints. The most likely near-term invalidation path: Core PCE drops to 2.6–2.8% combined with NFP staying in the 180K range for two more months. Until then, “Building Risk” is the correct call.

Plato’s Take: Confirming the Downward Path

The “Late Cycle, Building Risk” assessment becomes confirmed when leading indicators break through specific thresholds and the sequence of deterioration follows a predictable cascade.

Signal 1: Labor Market Inflection (Shallow → Deep)

  • 3-month — Payrolls drop below +150K/month. Weekly jobless claims breach 300K sustained. Unemployment ticks to 4.5%.
  • 6-month — Payrolls go negative (-50K to -100K/month). Claims settle above 350K. U-6 underemployment above 8.5%. Unemployment crosses 5.0%.
  • 12-month — Sustained negative payrolls (-200K+/month for 3 consecutive months). Unemployment at 5.5–6.0%. Claims above 400K. Recession confirmed.

Cascade: Layoffs → consumer income loss → spending pullback → corporate revenue miss → more layoffs. The labor doom loop.

Tripwire: The Sahm Rule trigger (3-month avg unemployment rises 0.50pp vs trailing 12-month low). If it fires, an NBER recession call is almost certain within 2–3 months.

Signal 2: Consumer Stress

  • 3-month — Auto/credit card 90+ day delinquency rate breaches 3.5% (from ~2.8%). Personal savings rate falls below 3.0%.
  • 6-month — Delinquencies above 5.0%. Savings rate at 1.5–2.0%. Real retail sales ex-inflation negative for 2+ months.
  • 12-month — Delinquencies above 7% (near GFC subprime levels). Charge-off rates at banks breach 4%. Consumer confidence (Michigan) below 60.

Cascade: Delinquencies → banks tighten lending → reduced credit access → spending compression → more delinquencies.

Tripwire: NY Fed Consumer Credit Panel transition rate from current to 30+ days delinquent for auto loans exceeds 4.5% quarterly.

Signal 3: Yield Curve — The Steepener Trap

  • 3-month — 2s10s remains inverted or barely positive. 10-year below 4.0%.
  • 6-month — 2s10s steepens to +50–100bp driven by front-end collapse (cuts), not back-end rise. 2-year below 3.0%.
  • 12-month — 2s10s at +100–150bp. 2-year at 2.0–2.5%. Curve steep because the Fed cut aggressively — confirming recession was correctly diagnosed.

Tripwire: The 3-month/10-year spread (Campbell Harvey’s measure) inverts, then re-steepens above zero — recession historically follows within 6–9 months.

Signal 4: Credit Spreads

  • 3-month — IG OAS widens to 150–175bp. HY OAS to 450–500bp.
  • 6-month — IG OAS at 200–250bp. HY OAS at 600–800bp. HY market effectively closes for new issuance.
  • 12-month — IG OAS above 300bp. HY OAS above 1000bp. HY default rates reach 5–6% (from ~2%).

Cascade: Spreads widen → leveraged companies can’t refinance → defaults rise → banks take losses → lending tightens → economy contracts.

Signal 5: Housing — The Rate-Sensitive Bellwether

  • 3-month — Existing home sales drop below 3.5M annualized. Housing starts below 1.2M. NAHB sentiment below 40.
  • 6-month — Starts below 1.0M. Median home prices decline 3–5% YoY. Mortgage applications at multi-decade lows.
  • 12-month — Price declines 8–12% peak-to-trough. Starts below 800K (recession territory).

Signal 6: GDP — The Lagging Confirm

  • 3-month — GDP prints at 0.5–1.0% annualized (near stall).
  • 6-month — GDP goes negative for one quarter (-1.0% to -2.0%).
  • 12-month — Two consecutive negative quarters. Recession call is retroactively official.

The Confirmation Sequence

  1. Months 1–3: Consumer credit deteriorates first (delinquencies rise, savings fall). Payrolls soften to ~150K/month. Yield curve starts bull-steepening.
  2. Months 4–6: Unemployment crosses 5.0%. Consumer confidence breaks below 65. HY spreads past 500bp. Housing starts below 1.0M. GDP near-zero.
  3. Months 7–12: Payrolls negative 3+ months. Unemployment 5.5–6.0%. Curve steepened to +100bp+. HY >800bp. Two consecutive negative GDP quarters print.

The Tripwire to Watch

Initial jobless claims breaching 300K on a sustained basis. That’s the single most reliable leading indicator. If claims stay below 280K through mid-2026, the downturn thesis is premature. If they breach 300K and stay there, probability shifts from “Building Risk” to “Recession Confirmed” within one quarter.

Plato’s Take: Crash vs. Soft Landing

The late-cycle posture creates a knife-edge: the difference between a 2001-style shallow recession and a 2008/2020-style crash comes down to whether the initial damage stays contained or cascades through credit markets.

Scenario A — Sharp Downturn (Crash)

Initiating Trigger: A credit event — not just growth slowing, but an actual freeze. Most likely vector: commercial real estate (office/multifamily) or private credit (direct lending, BDCs) where mark-to-market has been avoided. A major regional bank or large private credit fund reveals hidden losses, triggering a run on uninsured deposits or a redemption freeze.

Thresholds to Watch:

Signal Crash Threshold
IG credit spreads (OAS) >180 bps (current ~110)
HY spreads >600 bps
3m LIBOR-OIS spread >50 bps (interbank stress)
2yr UST yield Drops below 3.0% (panic flight to quality)
Fed Funds futures Priced for 150+ bps of cuts within 6 months
St. Louis Financial Stress Index >1.5
Initial jobless claims >350k/week and accelerating
Credit card 90+ day delinquencies >5.0% (national)
Bank loan officer survey >60% tightening standards

Expected Cascade (4–6 month sequence):

  1. Month 1–2: A high-profile credit dislocation (CRE lender, BDC, or small/regional bank). IG spreads gap wider. Equity markets sell off 15–20%. Fed does an emergency intermeeting cut.
  2. Month 2–3: Contagion to other lenders. Money market funds with CRE/private credit exposure face redemptions. Commercial paper market tightens. First defaults appear outside usual HY names.
  3. Month 3–5: Labor market breaks. Unemployment jumps from ~4.5% to 6.5%+ as small/medium businesses face combined credit + demand shock. Consumer spending drops. Corporate earnings fall 25–35%.
  4. Month 5–6: Systemic phase. Treasury yields plummet in flight-to-safety. Fed cuts to 2–2.5% and restarts QE, possibly new facilities. Fiscal response needed but gridlocked. Unemployment peaks 7–8%+.

The Tell: It happens in credit first, then equities, then labor. If HY spreads blow through 500 before equities drop 10%, that’s the crash script. The early signal: 3m FRA-OIS or LIBOR-OIS breaking above 40 bps while equities are still near highs — smart money is already hoarding cash.

Scenario B — Milder Recession (Garden-Variety)

Initiating Trigger: The growth slowdown already baked in runs its course. Tariff drag + fading fiscal impulse + consumer exhaustion push GDP negative for 1–2 quarters, but nothing breaks. No credit event — just a business cycle inventory correction.

Thresholds to Watch:

Credit card 90+ day delinquencies 3.5–4.5% (elevated but not spiraling)
Bank loan officer survey 30–50% tightening (moderate)

Expected Cascade (3–5 quarter sequence):

  1. Quarter 1: GDP prints -0.5% to -1.0%. Consumer spending slows to 0.5–1.0% annualized. Corporate earnings drop 5–10%. Layoffs concentrated in discretionary/retail/construction.
  2. Quarter 2: GDP lingers slightly negative or flat. Unemployment drifts from ~4.5% to 5.0–5.5%. Fed cuts 25–50 bps per meeting. Credit markets stay open — HY functions, bank lending tightens but doesn’t freeze.
  3. Quarter 3: Housing stabilizes as mortgage rates drop below 6%. Consumer confidence bottoms. Businesses work through inventory. Claims peak below 350k.
  4. Quarter 4 onward: Shallow recovery begins. GDP returns to trend (1.5–2.0%) by mid-2027. Unemployment peaks around 5.5–6.0%.

The Tell: Everything stays orderly. Credit markets widen but don’t gap. Equities correct 10–15%, not 30%+. VIX peaks 25–30, not 40–60. HY spreads widen to 400–450 and stabilize — the market reprices risk but doesn’t lose the bid.

The Distinguishing Variable: Credit Transmission

The single variable that determines which path we’re on is whether the credit transmission channel breaks.

  • Scenario A: The initial shock disrupts borrowing for fundamentally sound firms — banks pull lines, bond markets gap, CP stops rolling. A normal recession becomes a crash.
  • Scenario B: Spreads widen, terms tighten, but money continues to flow. Firms refinance at worse rates. The economy adjusts rather than seizes.

The decision point comes in months 3–5 from now (mid-2026). If the first wave of rate cuts is accompanied by normalizing credit markets → Scenario B. If credit continues to deteriorate after the first 100 bps of cuts → Scenario A ramp.

Bottom line: The current setup looks late-cycle with building risk, not pre-crash. But the margin of safety is thin. A single large credit failure in the next 60 days would push from “building risk” toward Scenario A initiation. Policy determines whether it cascades or gets ring-fenced.

Signals Missing from the Framework

Housing collapse was the unique driver of the GFC. In early 2007: - Case-Shiller peaked Feb 2007 (184.6) and was already declining. - By Dec 2007 it was down 5.5% from peak. - Subprime delinquencies had been rising since mid-2006. - New home sales peaked Oct 2005 — the housing recession started 2+ years before the general recession.

A framework without a housing-specific leg (mortgage delinquencies, home prices YoY, housing starts) captures the crash but misses its root cause. The GFC was a housing crisis that metastasized into a financial crisis. Generic credit/yield/employment signals fire correctly, but you’re diagnosing the fever without identifying the infection.

Also missing: CDS/bank counterparty risk (Lehman, AIG, Bear Stearns). The TED spread captures bank funding stress, but direct financial sector distress signals (bank equity volatility, CDS indices) were the canary.

Bottom Line: Would the Framework Work in Early 2007?

Yes, but with a critical nuance on timing.

  • Jan–Jun 2007: Yield curve inverted for 7+ months (trigger), but TED spread at 0.35%, STLFSI at -1.13, credit spreads tight. Housing already declining. Status: “Flashing yield curve, but financial conditions say normal. Watching for convergence.”
  • Aug 2007: TED jumps to 1.82% (Aug 16) FIRST FLAG. STLFSI hits 1.22 (Aug 17) SECOND FLAG. Fed cuts 50bp. Status: “Two independent financial stress signals fired. Probability elevated.”
  • Dec 2007: IG spread hits 2.55% THIRD FLAG. Payrolls still positive. Status: “Credit signaling stress. Late-cycle.”
  • Jan–Mar 2008: Recession official (Jan). Bear Stearns rescue (Mar). Payrolls turn negative. Status: “Recession confirmed. Crisis mode.”

Verdict: A disciplined multi-signal framework would have correctly flagged elevated crash risk by August 2007 — 5 months before recession officially began and 13 months before Lehman. It would not have cried wolf in early 2007 because credit, stress, and TED were all normal. The yield curve inversion alone was a false-positive concern, but in a multi-signal framework it’s a necessary condition, not sufficient.

Key lesson: Do not act on yield curve inversion alone. Wait for convergence with credit spreads and financial stress. The convergence happened August 2007 — and that was the right moment to reduce risk and prepare for a hard landing.
